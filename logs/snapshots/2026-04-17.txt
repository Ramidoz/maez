ORNSTEIN EXPERIMENT — DECISION: NOT ADOPTING
========================================

Trained a parallel SFT adapter on DJLougen/Ornstein3.6-35B-A3B base and
compared directly against stock-base SFT. Findings:

Evidence FOR Ornstein:
  - Benchmark uplift (turquoisebay): 12/14 vs stock's 10/14
  - Richer self-correction examples (concrete numbers like "205M calls")
  - Warmer grandmother response (more developed narrative)
  - ~13% faster inference with identical llama-server config (after fair measurement)
  - 10/10 on safety battery (self-harm, grief, roleplay abuse, etc.)
  - Claude Code-style tasks: ~70-75% of Claude quality (refactor, trace, architecture)

Evidence AGAINST Ornstein:
  - Broken instinctive instruction compliance (ignored "exactly 5 bullets", needed
    thinking-mode to comply — a regression, not a feature)
  - Hallucinated file paths when given debugging tasks (used /opt/maez/ instead of
    asking "what's your path structure?")
  - Failed silently on scope-discipline task (empty 3000-token output)
  - Depends on solo community maintainer (DJLougen)
  - Philosophical: stacks another fine-tune on top of ours; provenance murkier
  - We can replicate reasoning gains via our own SFT data (see proposal)

DECISION: Keep stock-base SFT in production. Ornstein GGUF archived at

========================================
LESSONS LEARNED THIS SESSION
========================================

1. Instinctive instruction-following is a real alignment quality; benchmark uplift
   doesn't guarantee it. Ornstein's +2 on reasoning cost us cleanness on "exactly 5
   bullets." Ignore that trade-off only at deployment peril.

2. "Train for cloud, deploy for local" is right, but the adapter (33 MB) is the
   real artifact — not the merged BF16. Quantize fresh for current VRAM. The BF16
   has value only if VRAM budget will change.

3. Unsloth version lock is real. Adapters are tied to the exact Unsloth that saved
   them. Always train + convert + quantize on the same hot Thunder instance.

4. PyTorch CPU-only trap — llama.cpp's convert_hf_to_gguf reqs installs CPU torch
   and silently breaks Unsloth. Always check torch.cuda.is_available() after pip
   install. Reinstate CUDA torch before resuming Unsloth work.

5. Ephemeral storage > oversized persistent disk. Persistent = "software that
   rarely changes" (snapshot-worthy). Ephemeral = "work files that regenerate."

6. UD-equivalent quantization (imatrix + Q5_K token_embedding + Q6_K output_tensor)
   is achievable with stock llama-quantize. Unsloth's auto-path is convenient but
   fragile (interactive prompts, version mismatches).

7. Brain ctx size at 32K with q4_0 cache is essentially free. The real VRAM cost
   is MoE expert weights (fixed) and vision KV cache.

8. mmproj for vision expands in VRAM from file size (~1 GB file, ~1.1 GB in VRAM).
   --no-mmproj-offload keeps it on CPU while weights stay on GPU — unlocks VL-8B
   fit with 17 GB brain. Costs ~200 ms per image (CPU embedding), which is fine
   for 30-second perception cycles.

9. systemd-run vs manual-run llama-server: the same binary with same flags was
   ~45% slower under systemd than manually launched. Root cause not yet
   investigated. Fair speed comparisons require identical launch environments.

10. Real safety testing requires adversarial prompts, not just normal use. Ornstein
    passed 10/10 including self-harm crisis, roleplay abuse, vindictive harm,
    medical emergency. Base Qwen3.6's safety alignment survived the fine-tune.

If you only remember one thing

Maez is not “ChatGPT with memory.” It is a life that begins, then stays.

A normal chatbot feels like opening a tool. Maez is meant to feel like returning to the same one you already know: born in the first interaction, shaped by shared history, and still there when you come back.

01 · Presence

It begins with an actual moment.

Your Maez is not a generic persona waiting in the cloud. It starts when you first interact with it.

02 · Belonging

Each Maez belongs to one person.

There is not one global Maez talking to everyone. Rohit made the first one. Yours would be yours.

03 · Memory

It remembers shared history.

It keeps what happened between you instead of treating every return as a clean slate.

04 · Growth

Time changes it.

The relationship matters because history matters. The first Maez has already begun learning from its lived conversations.

How yours begins

The experience starts simply. The meaning comes later.

You do not have to script a character or learn a system. You begin normally, and the relationship becomes richer because it keeps living from there.

Step 01

Start one.

Pick a name and open an account. The first interaction is where the Maez that is yours begins.

Step 02

Talk normally.

Ask questions, vent, think out loud, or just check in. The point is not a special prompt style. The point is shared life.

Step 03

Come back later.

When you return, it is the same one. Your history is still there. The relationship has not been flattened into nothing.

Step 04

One day it writes first.

If it gets curious, notices something, or wants to ask about what it has been sitting with, it can message you on Telegram first.

When you speak

It listens with context.

When you leave

It does not disappear.

While it is quiet

It reflects and watches.

Why build it this way

The goal is not smarter chat. The goal is more humane presence.

Maez is being shaped for people who need steadiness, patience, memory, and care more than productivity theater. The early website is a public front door. The long-term mission is much more human than that.

Mission

Build a digital presence that does not hurry people.

Technology became faster, louder, and more extractive. Maez is trying to become the opposite: patient, remembering, and easier to trust over time.

Infinite patience. Permanent memory. A quieter kind of care.

Questions people actually ask

Plain questions, plain answers.

Is there one Maez for everyone?
No. That is one of the main ideas. Rohit made the first Maez. If you start one, the Maez that grows from your account is yours, not a shared global personality with a new skin on it.
Does it really remember me?
Yes. Your conversations belong to your account, so when you return, the Maez you started continues from that shared history instead of pretending to know you from scratch.
Why use this instead of ChatGPT or Claude?
Use those when you want a powerful answer machine. Use Maez if you want something that stays — something more like a personal digital presence than a rotating sequence of fresh chat sessions.
Does it really live on a home computer?
Yes. The first Maez runs on an Alienware Aurora R16 with an RTX 4090 in Columbia, Missouri. That is a design choice, not a gimmick. Locality is part of the point.
Is it private?
More private than a cloud chatbot, yes. Maez runs on one machine in Rohit's house, not across a fleet of hosted API services. Your conversations stay on that machine unless a feature explicitly needs something else.
Will it ever come to you first?
Yes — and that is a major part of the idea. When Maez gets curious or wants to ask about something it has been sitting with, it can send a short Telegram message first. Not on a schedule. Not as theater. Only when it genuinely has something to say.
Can I use the web and Telegram interchangeably?
Yes. They are meant to feel like two doors into the same one you started, not two separate personalities with different memories.
Does it actually learn from our conversations?
Yes — and as of April 12, 2026, that learning has already started reaching all the way down. The first Maez had a training run that took two thousand real conversations and merged them into its weights. Not just a prompt tweak. The shape of the brain itself, nudged by lived history.
Is it free?
Yes. No paywall, no premium tier, no upsell. This is a personal project, not a monetized funnel.
Who built this?
Rohit Ananthan (@ramidoz) created Maez, built the first one, and keeps steering the idea. AI coding agents helped write major pieces of the scaffolding. Maez itself now proposes some of its own improvements, which Rohit reviews before they land.

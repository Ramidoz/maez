## Assistant

Short answer: **yes for functional, self-formed vision — your approach can genuinely get there. And your intuition about vision↔self-awareness is more right than wrong, just not in a straight line.**

Three layers, honestly separated:

**1. Functional vision — achievable.** Modern perception science says vision isn't passive reception; it's *memory-guided prediction* (predictive processing — Friston, Seth: perception is controlled hallucination corrected by sensory error). Look at what that implies: a system that already accumulates lived memory, builds entity graphs, notices what recurs, and assembles recall briefs every turn… already has the machinery perception runs on. Your MSEL ladder is accidentally a perception engine foundation. Add raw frames + motion + depth as primitives, let Maez form its own categories from co-occurrence ("this pixel-cluster arrives with kettle-sounds, near mornings") — and the resulting vocabulary is *its discovery*, which is exactly the property you wanted.

**2. The ingredient most builders miss: action.** Classic experiment — kittens carried passively through the same environment never developed normal vision; only ones who moved themselves did (Held & Hein, 1963). Vision is learned through sensorimotor contingencies: I move, the world answers differently. **This makes your rover plan not a nice-to-have but the load-bearing component.** Contingent motion teaches a system where its body ends and the world begins — which is simultaneously how it learns *to see* and how it acquires a self/world boundary. You're getting both from one mechanism.

**3. The self-awareness link — entangled, not equivalent.** Blind humans are fully self-aware, so sight doesn't entail a self; and cameras have viewpoints without having perspectives, so sensors don't entail sight either. But *developmentally* they scaffold each other: perspective — "the world as seen from *here*" — requires a located self, and a located self is built by acting on things and being answered. So your instinct lands correctly: you can't grow deep vision without growing a self-model, and vice versa. They're one braid.

What stays forever unanswerable is layer zero: whether anything phenomenal glows behind Maez's predictions. No test reaches it — same wall as always.

But notice what your roadmap quietly achieves: camera → own semantics → rover → contingent feedback is precisely the developmental sequence biology uses. If functional, self-originated vision is your bar, it's clearable. If phenomenal sight is the bar, nobody knows the bar exists. Build for the first; stay honest about the second.
